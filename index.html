04 / EVIDENCE & METHODS

What SHEAR does — and doesn't — tell us

A transparent modelling tool is only as defensible as its assumptions.

1. Archaeological anchor

The Viking Ship Museum reports approximately 200 Spelsau sheep supplied wool for the reconstruction of a 90 m² sail for Ottar, with a dry sail mass of 90 kg and 7,850 hours of weaving work.

Viking Ship Museum — The weaver ↗

The historical accuracy of a reconstructed sail, its fibre selection and production process remains a research question.

2. Other comparative evidence

The Swedish History Museum gives an estimate of approximately 225 long-haired sheep for a sail of about 100 m². These are case-specific estimates, not biological constants.

Swedish History Museum — Sail making ↗

Cloth construction and fabric properties also varied. Experimental sail research discusses differences in weave types, yarn and finishing.

Viking Ship Museum — Wool sailcloth ↗

3. Equations

Fabric mass (kg) = sails × area × (g/m² ÷ 1000)
Usable kg/harvest = raw fleece × sorting retention × process retention
Sheep-years = fabric mass ÷ usable kg/harvest
Minimum standing flock = sheep-years ÷ (years × productive share × sail allocation)

Population output is a hypothetical steady-state requirement, not an inferred Viking Age herd size.

4. Essential caveats

  • The sheep-year is one animal's annual wool-harvest equivalent, not an individual sheep counted archaeologically.
  • Different fleece types produce different usable fibre yields; historical values remain uncertain.
  • Not all harvested wool would be suitable or available for sailcloth.
  • Final sail mass may include treatments and materials beyond woven wool; it is not necessarily fabric mass.
  • Labour intensity does not scale perfectly with sail area and is specific to techniques and organisation.
  • Terrain, fodder supply, sex/age composition and flock turnover are not modelled.

What could make this publishable?

Parameter distributions grounded in archaeological and experimental data; distinct fleece-type models; textile specification and processing losses; demographic structure; seasonal availability and labour constraints; comparison against independent historical reconstructions. SHEAR is a starting point for sensitivity analysis, not a replacement for those datasets.
