04 / ASSUMPTIONS & EVIDENCE

Model documentation

Interpretation, provenance and methodological limits.

Demographic mass balance

Each flock cohort contributes its standing-flock share multiplied by its annual collected raw wool yield. All wool is pooled and subjected to fibre selection, processing retention and target allocation. The output is the average constant-composition population needed for the textile target.

Raw kg/head/year = Σ (cohort share × annual raw yield)
Target kg/head/year = raw × selection × processing × allocation
Flock = cloth kg ÷ (years × target kg/head/year)

We assume one annual accounting period, not a specified historical shearing schedule. An annual collection yield may contain multiple harvests if empirically justified.

Wool-producing animal-years

This is a normalised fibre supply equivalent: cloth mass divided by usable kilograms from the average actively producing animal (before wool allocation). It is not identical to a count of distinct historical sheep. The flock estimate separately includes juveniles and allocation away from the target textile.

Raw wool totals reflect what the modelled flock harvests during the production horizon. Wool diverted to other products is counted in harvested raw wool, but not the target textile.

Fleece types are hypotheses

Fleece composition, body size, sex, age, breed, selection and harvesting practices affect usable wool. The presets only illustrate alternative fibre-sorting strategies and are not empirical distributions for Viking Age flocks.

Experiments with primitive sheep show that fine underwool and coarser overcoat may be separated and combined for different textiles.

Calibration is not validation

The Viking Ship Museum documents a 90 m² sail for Ottar using wool from approximately 200 Spelsau sheep, each yielding around 500 g usable wool. This does not itself specify the raw fleece mass, historical demographic structure, or a universal sailcloth mass.

Viking Ship Museum — Ottar ↗

Danish fibre studies suggest changes in fleece qualities and textile processing through time, so no single profile should be universalised.

Skals et al. 2024 — 3,000-year wool study ↗

Probability ≠ evidence

Monte Carlo draws represent your assumed uncertainty, not sampled archaeological variation. Triangular distributions peak at the nominal value. Uniform distributions treat every value between bounds equally. Percentile bands are conditional on the stated assumptions and should not be reported as statistical confidence intervals.

Model inputs are treated as independent except for juvenile share, which necessarily changes adult cohort proportions. Production, births, deaths, survival, pasture availability and fibre seasonality are not explicitly simulated.

Reproducibility & export

CSV output contains all deterministic input values and central results. Monte Carlo draw exports include each sampled input and predicted flock size. JSON backups preserve notes, distribution settings, seed, saved scenarios and app version. The simulation algorithm and seed are documented in the downloaded source.

Do not treat these projections as demonstrated historical flock sizes without external constraints and validation.
